\begin{abstract}
World action models (WAMs) predict how a scene will evolve and condition their actions on this predicted future.
Deploying them on a robot's onboard computer calls for 4-bit weights, activations and key--value caches (W4A4KV4).
Existing 4-bit quantizers for WAMs preserve the generated action, and estimate sensitivity one denoising step at a time.
However, they fail to preserve the fidelity of the predicted future: once the action pathway is protected, the remaining action error enters almost entirely through a degraded future that none of them deliberately protects.
We propose \method, a latent-aware quantization framework that preserves the predicted future where it is written, stored and read.
Specifically, we introduce a reader metric, which measures future error by its effect on the action, and a read count, which weights each rounding error by the number of denoising steps that read it.
Together they give the allocation of precision over weights, activations and caches a closed-form solution.
We further develop gaze-guided refinement, which gives the tokens the action attends to a second 4-bit pass in the denoising loop and in the cache.
All weights stay at 4 bits, with no high-precision branch and no per-task recalibration.
We evaluate \method on two WAMs, both in simulation and in real-world deployment.
On the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, it reaches \est{75.8}\% and \est{83.0}\% average success on Fast-WAM and Cosmos-Policy, within \est{1.8} percentage points of the 16-bit models and \est{2.1}--\est{3.2} points above the strongest baseline with 16-bit caches, while reducing weight memory by up to \est{3.5}$\times$.
On an AgileX PiPER arm, it succeeds in \est{59} of \est{75} real-world trials, compared with \est{47} for the strongest baseline and \est{62} for the 16-bit model.
\end{abstract}
