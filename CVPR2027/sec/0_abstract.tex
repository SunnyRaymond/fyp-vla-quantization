\begin{abstract}
World action models (WAMs) jointly predict how a scene will evolve and how to act, conditioning their actions on the predicted future.
Deploying them on a robot's onboard computer is memory-bound (the 6B-parameter Fast-WAM needs 12\,GB for its BF16 weights alone), which calls for ultra-low-bit weight quantization, a regime where scalar quantizers collapse and vector quantization (VQ) is the method of choice.
However, existing VQ methods, developed for language models, fail to preserve the fidelity of the predicted future: they reconstruct each layer or the final output, and nothing in their objectives deliberately protects the future on which the action depends.
We propose \method, a reader-aware vector quantization framework that makes future-latent fidelity an explicit quantization target.
\method (i) measures future-latent error in the metric through which the policy reads the future, (ii) distributes a fixed bit budget according to each module's measured impact on future fidelity, (iii) initializes the codes with trellis-coded rounding, which natively supports the resulting mixed bit widths, and (iv) calibrates the shared decode tables toward action and future-latent fidelity at no extra storage.
We evaluate \method across \XX WAMs, both in simulation and in real-world deployment.
On 840 paired LIBERO-Plus episodes, 2-bit \method raises Fast-WAM's success rate from \XX\% (QTIP, the strongest 2-bit baseline) to \XX\%, within \XX points of BF16 (77.6\%).
On an AgileX PiPER arm, 2-bit \method succeeds in \XX/\XX real-world trials across three tasks, compared with \XX/\XX for QTIP and \XX/\XX for BF16, with \XX\,GB of weights.
\end{abstract}
