\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Ultra-low-bit weight quantization makes a WAM lose the fidelity of its predicted future, and most of its action error follows from this loss.
\method makes the predicted future an explicit quantization target: it measures future error as the reader sees it, spends bits where they preserve the future, and calibrates the shared decode tables toward action and future fidelity.
At the same bits per weight as existing vector quantizers, this recovers closed-loop success in simulation and on a real robot.
When compressing a WAM, the predicted future is not an intermediate quantity that can be sacrificed.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The bit allocation relies on measuring each module group's impact on future fidelity; in models whose modules have similar impact, it reduces to rate--distortion allocation.
The reader metric linearizes the reader at the full-precision future.
We quantize weights only and keep activations in BF16.
Our real-world evaluation covers three tasks on one robot.
